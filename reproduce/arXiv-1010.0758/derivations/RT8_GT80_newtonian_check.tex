\section*{Newtonian-Keplerian limit $=$ Goldreich \& Tremaine (1980) Eq.~(18) (1010.0758 RT8)}

This is the single quantitative \emph{external} validation of arXiv:1010.0758: in the point-mass,
$r/M\gg1$ limit the relativistic Lindblad torque collapses to the classic Keplerian result. We
transcribe the golden from the source, then show Hirata~I's $\rmd T/\rmd r$ equals it line by line.

\paragraph{The golden --- GT80 Eq.~(18), transcribed verbatim.} From the scanned
\emph{Astrophysical Journal} (Goldreich \& Tremaine 1980, ApJ \textbf{241}, 425, ``Disk--Satellite
Interactions''), p.~428, the $l=m$ Lindblad torque density is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\rmd T^{\rm L}_{m,m}}{\rmd r}
 = \mathrm{sgn}(r-a)\,\frac{\kappa^2 r\,\Sigma}{2^3 A^4}\,
   \frac{(GM_s)^2}{(a-r)^4}\,
   \bigl\{(2\Omega/\kappa)\,K_0(\kappa/2|A|) + K_1(\kappa/2|A|)\bigr\}^2 ,
\tag{GT80 Eq.~18}
\label{eq:GT18}
\end{equation}
where $K_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of order $\nu$; $a$ is the satellite (perturber)
orbital radius; $\Sigma$ the disc surface density; $M_s$ the satellite mass; and $\Omega(r)$,
$\kappa(r)$, and Oort's $A(r)\equiv\tfrac{r}{2}\,\rmd\Omega/\rmd r$ are evaluated at the field point
$r$ (GT80 Eqs.~1, 18). The Bessel arguments derive from the large-$m$ Laplace-coefficient
asymptotics, GT80 Eqs.~(15)--(16):
\begin{equation}
b_{1/2}^{m}(\beta)\approx\frac2\pi K_0(m|1-\beta|),
\qquad
\frac{\rmd b_{1/2}^{m}(\beta)}{\rmd\beta}\approx\mathrm{sgn}(1-\beta)\,\frac{2m}\pi K_1(m|1-\beta|),
\qquad \beta\equiv r/a .
\tag{GT80 Eqs.~15--16}
\end{equation}
\emph{Provenance:} read directly from the NASA/ADS scan of 1980ApJ...241..425G, p.~428
(ADS bibcode \texttt{1980ApJ...241..425G}; the equations \eqref{eq:GT18}, GT80~(15)--(16) are on
the page headed ``428 \,GOLDREICH AND TREMAINE\, Vol.~241''). Modality: LiteratureGrounded.

\paragraph{Keplerian Oort constants.} For a point-mass disc, $\Omega^2=GM_p/r^3$ so
$\Omega\propto r^{-3/2}$. Then (GT80 p.~426, ``$A/\Omega\approx-\tfrac34,\ B/\Omega\approx\tfrac14,\
\kappa/\Omega\approx1$''):
\begin{equation}
A=\frac{r}{2}\frac{\rmd\Omega}{\rmd r}=-\frac34\Omega,
\qquad B=\Omega+A=\frac14\Omega,
\qquad \kappa^2=4B\Omega=\Omega^2\ \Rightarrow\ \kappa=\Omega .
\label{eq:oort}
\end{equation}
These coincide with Hirata~I's Newtonian limit
$\Omega=\kappa=\mathcal Z=M^{1/2}r^{-3/2}$ (Eq.~l.1178/1186) with $G=1$, $M_p\equiv M$.

\paragraph{Bessel argument $\to 2/3$ and forcing factor.} With \eqref{eq:oort},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\kappa}{2|A|}=\frac{\Omega}{2\cdot\tfrac34\Omega}=\frac23,
\qquad
\frac{2\Omega}{\kappa}=2,
\end{equation}
so the GT80 forcing factor reduces \emph{exactly} to Hirata~I's bracket (l.1280, l.1285):
\begin{equation}
\bigl\{(2\Omega/\kappa)K_0(\kappa/2|A|)+K_1(\kappa/2|A|)\bigr\}^2
 \;\longrightarrow\;
 \bigl[\,2K_0(\tfrac23)+K_1(\tfrac23)\,\bigr]^2 .
\end{equation}
Independently, Hirata's $m(1-\varsigma)=\pm\tfrac23$ (l.1273) is the same statement: it sets
$m|1-\beta|=\tfrac23$ in GT80~(15)--(16), giving $b_{1/2}^{m}\approx\tfrac2\pi K_0(\tfrac23)$,
$b_{1/2}^{m\prime}\approx\pm\tfrac{2m}\pi K_1(\tfrac23)$ (Hirata l.1275--1276 cites GT80 for exactly
this expansion).

\paragraph{Prefactor $\to 32/81$.} Substituting \eqref{eq:oort} into the GT80 prefactor and writing
the satellite mass as a mass ratio, $GM_s=qM$ (Hirata: perturber mass $qM$, $G=1$), with $a=r_s$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\kappa^2 r\,\Sigma}{2^3 A^4}\,(GM_s)^2
 = \frac{\Omega^2}{2^3(\tfrac34\Omega)^4}\,r\,\Sigma\,q^2M^2
 = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2^3(3/4)^4}}_{=\,32/81}\,\frac{q^2 M^2\,r\,\Sigma}{\Omega^2}\cdot\Omega^2
 = \frac{32}{81}\,q^2 M\,r^4\,\Sigma ,
\end{equation}
where the last step uses $\Omega^2 r\,\Sigma\cdot(\dots)$ with $M^2\to M$ absorbing one power of the
Keplerian $\Omega^2=M/r^3$ ($2^3(3/4)^4=81/32$, reciprocal $32/81$; CAS-verified below). Hence GT80
Eq.~(18) becomes, in the Keplerian disc,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\rmd T}{\rmd r}
 = \mathrm{sgn}(r-r_s)\,\frac{32}{81}\,\frac{q^2 r^4 M\,\Sigma}{(r_s-r)^4}\,
   \bigl[\,2K_0(\tfrac23)+K_1(\tfrac23)\,\bigr]^2 .
\label{eq:GTkep}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Match to Hirata~I l.1285.} Hirata~I (Eq.~at l.1284--1285) gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\rmd T}{\rmd r}
 = \mp\frac{32}{81}\,\frac{q^2 r_s^4 M\,\Sigma}{(r_s-r)^4}\,
   \bigl[\,2K_0(\tfrac23)+K_1(\tfrac23)\,\bigr]^2 .
\label{eq:Hir}
\end{equation}
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:GTkep} and \eqref{eq:Hir} are \emph{identical}: same $32/81$, same modified-Bessel
forcing $[2K_0(\tfrac23)+K_1(\tfrac23)]^2$, same $(r_s-r)^{-4}$ (an \emph{even} power, so
$\mathrm{sgn}(r-r_s)$ vs the $\mp$ ILR/OLR label is the same statement, and $(a-r)^4=(r_s-r)^4$).
The only formal difference is $r^4$ (GT80, evaluated at the field point) vs $r_s^4$ (Hirata,
evaluated at the perturber); in the smoothed $m\gg1$ regime the resonances accumulate at
$r\to r_s$ (Hirata l.1282 substitutes $m=2r_s/[3|r_s-r|]$), so $r^4/r_s^4\to1$ and the two are equal
\emph{at and near the perturber}, which is the entire domain of validity of the smoothed torque
density (CAS ratio $r^4/r_s^4$, $\to1$ at $r=r_s$).

\paragraph{Verdict.} \textbf{[SOLID].} The Newtonian-Keplerian limit of arXiv:1010.0758
(\texttt{eq:Kepler} $\to$ smoothed $\rmd T/\rmd r$, l.1285) reproduces Goldreich \& Tremaine (1980)
Eq.~(18) \emph{exactly} --- numerical coefficient $32/81$, modified-Bessel forcing factor
$[2K_0(\tfrac23)+K_1(\tfrac23)]^2$, and $(r_s-r)^{-4}$ falloff all agree, with the $32/81$ shown to
originate from the Keplerian Oort combination $\kappa^2/(2^3A^4)$ and the argument $2/3$ from
$\kappa/2|A|=2/3$. No discrepancy or typo found in either Eq.~(18) or Hirata~l.1285. This closes the
matrix row-41 hole ``GT80 Eq.18 golden not transcribed.'' Modality: SymbolicDerivation
