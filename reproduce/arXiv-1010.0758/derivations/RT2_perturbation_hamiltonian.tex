\section*{Linear perturbation Hamiltonian (1010.0758 RT2)}

Take $t$ as the independent variable, so the Hamiltonian is $H=-p_t$ with $p_t$ fixed by the mass-shell
$g^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta=-\mu^2$ at fixed spatial momenta $p_i$. Varying the mass-shell,
\begin{equation}
  \delta g^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta + 2g^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha\,\delta p_\beta = 0 ,
\end{equation}
and restricting the last term to $\beta=t$ (since $p_i$ are held fixed) gives
\begin{equation}
  \delta p_t = -\frac{1}{2p^t}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta
             = \frac{h^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta}{2p^t},
\end{equation}
using $\delta g^{\alpha\beta}=-g^{\alpha\kappa}g^{\beta\lambda}\delta g_{\kappa\lambda}=-h^{\alpha\beta}$
(the negative of $h$ with raised indices). Hence the linear-in-$h$ perturbation Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\,H_1 = \frac{h^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta}{2p^t}\,}\qquad(\texttt{eq:H1}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Verification.} For a random near-Minkowski inverse metric $g^{\alpha\beta}$, a random small
symmetric $h_{\alpha\beta}$ (raised to $h^{\alpha\beta}$), and momenta $p_\alpha$ on the unperturbed shell,
shifting $p_t\to p_t+\delta p_t$ with $\delta p_t=h^{\alpha\beta}p_\alpha p_\beta/(2p^t)$ and the spatial
$p_i$ fixed leaves the \emph{perturbed} shell $(g^{\alpha\beta}-h^{\alpha\beta})p'_\alpha p'_\beta+\mu^2$
nonzero only at $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$: over $h\in\{10^{-3},\dots,10^{-6}\}$ the residual$/h^2$ is constant
($\approx1.2\text{–}3\times10^2$), confirming $H_1$ is exact at linear order. \textbf{[SOLID]} (data computed
by the Julia port).
